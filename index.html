How it started

When I was 11 I installed an app called Sky Map on my dad's phone (I think that was the name). You point it at the sky and it tells you what everything is, which was cool. But the interest actually came when I saw it had some preset images, one of which was the Eagle Nebula, and when I first saw it I fell in love.

And with the stubbornness of the 11-year-old I was, I decided that this is what I would do. That I would look at the stars.

Research

Below is what I've worked on so far.

  • Published · ApJ

    A black hole with a heartbeat

    GRS 1915+105 is a black hole that flares every 50 to 100 seconds.

  • Published · ApJ

    Looking for two black holes orbiting each other

    If two supermassive black holes are orbiting each other, the light should go up and down with the orbit.

  • Preprint

    Can a fast X-ray fit tell when it's wrong?

    Neural networks can fit an X-ray spectrum in milliseconds, which is great, as long as the data looks like what they trained on.

  • Preprint

    One star or two?

    A white dwarf next to a normal star adds some extra blue light to the spectrum, so you can find these pairs in Gaia data.

  • Side project · stopped, might come back

    Could something alive be made of plasma?

    If something really lived on the surface of the Sun, it would probably have to be made of the stellar plasma too.
